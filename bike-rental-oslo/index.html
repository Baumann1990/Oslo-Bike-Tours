Where to Ride a Gravel Bike in Oslo

The Nordmarka starts at the end of metro line 5: 1,700 square kilometres of gravel roads running north to lakes, ridgelines and cabins serving coffee and waffles. Our journal covers it in detail:

One honest note: the forest trails are unmarked for visitors. Bring offline maps and plan conservatively for daylight.

FAQ

Common questions

Where do I collect the bike?

You can collect at Sognsvann (end of metro line 5 — right on the forest edge), Kjelsås (end of tram line 11), Huseby (two minutes' walk from Montebello on metro line 3, where the Mærradalen gravel route up to Bogstad begins) or Oslo Central Station. All four are included in the price. We can also deliver the bike to your hotel, AirBnB or any Oslo address for NOK 399. Select your preferred option in the booking form and we confirm the exact time and meeting point within 24 hours.

What is included?

A helmet is included with every rental, and every bike is checked and serviced between rentals. Tell us your height when you book and the right frame size will be ready.

What's the difference between the two gravel bikes?

The Premium Gravel (NOK 1 399/day) has a carbon frame, carbon wheels with tubeless tyres and hydraulic disc brakes — lighter, faster and more forgiving over a long forest day. The regular Gravel (NOK 899 first day) has an aluminium frame and disc brakes, and handles exactly the same terrain at a friendlier price.

How fit do I need to be for the forest on an e-bike?

Any fitness level works — both e-bikes take the Nordmarka's climbing out of the equation. The Premium E-Bike (NOK 899 first day) covers a normal forest day; the Long Range E-Bike (NOK 1 199 first day) carries the biggest battery in the fleet and is built for full-day rides to the remote lakes and cabins most day-trippers never reach.

Do the e-bikes handle the Nordmarka terrain?

Yes. Most of the Nordmarka forest's gravel terrain is accessible on our e-bikes — their wide tyres handle the loose gravel and forest roads, and the motor assistance takes care of the climbs. They are built for exactly this kind of riding.

Can I rent a gravel bike for several days?

Yes — multi-day rentals are our speciality and the best way to explore the Nordmarka forest properly. There's no maximum; rent for a weekend, a week, or your whole trip. Put your dates in the enquiry form and we'll confirm availability and multi-day pricing.

The Nordmarka forest can be hard to navigate. Do you do guided tours?

Yes. The trails are unmarked for visitors and the best routes take local knowledge, so many people prefer not to handle the navigation and logistics themselves. Our private guided tours take all of that out of the equation — a local guide leads the way and shows you the best of the forest, with hotel pickup and bike rental included. Tours start from NOK 890 per person, with rental included from NOK 349. See our private tours.

Is a gravel bike the right choice for the Nordmarka?

Yes — it's the best bike for the terrain. The Nordmarka forest is criss-crossed with gravel roads and forest tracks that are too rough for a road bike and too open for a mountain bike. A gravel bike handles it all: fast and efficient on the longer gravel roads, stable enough on the loose technical sections. A city hybrid will slow you down and tire you out. If you want to ride the forest properly, rent a gravel bike.

Where is the best place to start a gravel bike ride in Oslo?

Sognsvann — the last stop on metro line 5, fifteen minutes from central Oslo. You step off the train, pick up your rental gravel bike, and the forest gravel roads start immediately. No riding through traffic, no wasted time. From Sognsvann you can head north on the Ring 4 loop (45 km), push deeper into the Nordmarka to Kikut or Skjennungen, or keep it shorter and loop back along the lakeside tracks. It's the single best access point to Oslo's gravel riding.
